Overview

Welcome to the self-assessment portal for Class 5 and Class 6 (Probability Rules, Conditional Probability, and Statistical Independence). These six practice activities develop fluency in calculating event probabilities and in selecting the correct probability rule for a given problem.

Each section contains 10 short-answer questions. Only one question is shown at a time, and the complete question bank appears in a different random order each session.

Note📋 Practice Structure
  1. Class 5 — Probability Rules: Probability of an event, probability of a union, and the complementary theorem.
  2. Class 6 — Conditional Probability and Independence: Conditional probability from frequency tables, the multiplication rule for intersections, and statistical independence.
  3. Randomized Practice: Each section presents its 10 questions one at a time in a different random order each round.
  4. Flexible Probability Entry: Numerical probabilities may be entered as fractions, decimals, or percentages.
  5. Immediate Feedback: Check each response, review the correct answer and explanation, and monitor your score.

How the Practice Activities Work

  1. Open a section using its corresponding button.
  2. The 10 questions are randomly ordered at the beginning of each round.
  3. Only one question is displayed at a time.
  4. Enter your response and select Check Answer to receive immediate feedback.
  5. For numerical probabilities, you may enter an equivalent fraction, decimal, or percentage.
  6. Use Show Solution when you need to review the expected answer and reasoning.
  7. Continue until every question in the section has appeared once.
  8. At the end of the round, review your score and restart for a newly randomized order.

Instructions for Students

  1. Self-Paced Practice: These activities are intended for self-study and concept review. You may attempt each section as many times as you like.
  2. Identify the Event First: Translate words such as or, and, given that, and not into the appropriate event operation before calculating.
  3. Show Your Own Work: The quiz records only the final answer, so write the relevant formula and intermediate calculations separately.
  4. Use Sufficient Precision: When entering a decimal approximation, use at least four decimal places for nonterminating values.
  5. Interpret the Condition Carefully: In a conditional probability, the event after “given that” determines the restricted denominator.
  6. Questions & Feedback: If you encounter an unexpected result or need further clarification, bring your question to class or office hours.
