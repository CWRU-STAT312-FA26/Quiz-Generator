STAT 312: Class 1 Concepts

True / False Self-Assessment

1. Definition of Statistics
Statistics is defined solely as the numerical collection of raw facts and does not involve providing a measure of confidence in conclusions.
2. Population vs. Sample
A sample is the complete group of all individuals we are interested in studying, while a population is a small subset examined directly.
3. Parameter vs. Statistic
A population parameter is a fixed value that is typically unknown, whereas a sample statistic can be computed from collected data and used to infer population characteristics.
4. Data Collection Methods
Observational and retrospective studies can reveal statistical associations, but a designed experiment with randomization is required to establish a direct causal relationship.
5. Measures of Location (Mean vs. Median)
The sample mean utilizes all numerical values in the dataset but is sensitive to extreme outliers, whereas the sample median is resistant to outliers.
6. Measures of Variability (Sample Variance)
When calculating the sample variance \(s^2\), the sum of squared deviations \(\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2\) is divided by \(n\) rather than \(n - 1\).
