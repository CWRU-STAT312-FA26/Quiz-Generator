Type I Error, Type II Error, and Power

Calculate error areas, then distinguish them from an observed-data p-value.

Study guide: error probabilities

Use the proposed cutoffs for alpha and beta. Use the observed sample mean for its separate p-value.

X‾∼N(μ,σ2/n),SE=σ/n\bar X\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2/n),\quad SE=\sigma/\sqrt n.

Alpha is rejection area under the null. Beta is nonrejection area under the specified alternative. For a two-sided rule with nonrejection interval [L,U][L,U],

α=Pμ0(X‾<L)+Pμ0(X‾>U)\alpha=P_{\mu_0}(\bar X<L)+P_{\mu_0}(\bar X>U), β(μa)=Pμa(L≤X‾≤U)\beta(\mu_a)=P_{\mu_a}(L\le\bar X\le U).

Keep intermediate precision. When given a variance, take its square root first. A normalcdf calculation on the sample-mean scale takes the standard error as its SD argument.
