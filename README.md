### Case Coverage

Chapters 1-3 each contain four main problems:

1. Two discrete random variables with a **joint PMF table**.
2. Two discrete random variables with a **joint PMF formula**.
3. Two continuous random variables with **rectangular support**, whose bounds do not depend on the other variable.
4. Two continuous random variables with **triangular support**, whose integration bounds depend on the other variable, following Class 12 Practice Example 1.

Chapter 4 contains three main problems: a joint PMF table, a continuous joint PDF on a rectangle, and a continuous joint PDF on a triangle. Its statements test independence and its connection with conditional probabilities.

### Learning Objectives and Worked Solutions

- **Joint:** Read or sum PMF entries; integrate a PDF over rectangles and sloping event regions; split integration bounds when needed.
- **Marginal:** Sum or integrate out the other variable. Students derive the complete marginal PMF/PDF on paper, then enter the requested probability. Solutions include formulas, support, and normalization checks.
- **Conditional:** Form a conditional PMF/PDF using the appropriate marginal denominator, keep the restricted support, and calculate an event probability. For exact continuous conditioning, use a density ratio.
- **Independence:** Compare the joint PMF/PDF with the product of marginals, and compare conditional with marginal probabilities. Rectangular support alone does not imply statistical independence; a formula that omits one variable may still describe dependent variables when its support couples them.

The continuous rectangular example uses f(x,y) = (x+y)/3 on 0 < x < 1, 0 < y < 2. The triangular example uses f(x,y) = 6x on 0 < x < 1, 0 < y < 1-x, as in the lecture's first practice example. Probabilities come from integration, not PDF heights.

### Student Instructions and Quiz Behavior

1. Open a chapter. The main problems are shuffled without replacement at the beginning of a round; linked subquestions stay in order.
2. Write the event, formula, support, and intermediate calculations separately. For numerical questions, enter only the final probability.
3. Round decimal answers to **four decimal places**. Equivalent fractions, percentages, and scientific notation are accepted. Keep full precision until the final step.
4. In the independence chapter, select **True** or **False**.
5. Select **Check Answer** for one scored attempt per subquestion. Blank or invalid input does not consume an attempt. **Show Solution** reveals the answer and earns no point.
6. Review the worked explanation, then advance. The score counts subquestions, and the progress bar tracks completed subquestions.
7. Finish the chapter and select **Start a New Randomized Round** to reset the score and reshuffle the main problems.

### Chapter 1: Conceptual Properties

The ten statements cover independence versus zero covariance; covariance under scaling, shifting, and addition; the scale dependence of covariance; the sign change of correlation under a negative rescaling; the failure of additivity for correlation; zero correlation versus nonlinear dependence; correlation versus causation; and the need for a scatterplot to inspect curvature and influential outliers.

Finite second moments are assumed throughout. Pearson correlation additionally requires strictly positive variances. The chapter contains five true and five false statements, each with an explanation or counterexample.

### Chapter 2: Six Joint Distributions

| Case | Joint distribution supplied | Main problems |
|---|---|---:|
| A | Joint PMF table for two discrete random variables | 2 |
| B | Joint PMF formula for two discrete random variables | 2 |
| C | Joint PDF with rectangular support and fixed integration bounds | 2 |

Each main problem has **eleven linked subquestions**, in this order:

1. Calculate E(XY) from the joint PMF/PDF.
2. Derive the marginal PMF/PDF of X on paper, then enter E(X).
3. Derive the marginal PMF/PDF of Y on paper, then enter E(Y).
4. Calculate Cov(X,Y) = E(XY) - E(X)E(Y).
5. Interpret the covariance (**four-choice question**).
6. Calculate E(X²) from the marginal PMF/PDF of X.
7. Calculate E(Y²) from the marginal PMF/PDF of Y.
8. Calculate Var(X) = E(X²) - [E(X)]².
9. Calculate Var(Y) = E(Y²) - [E(Y)]².
10. Calculate Cor(X,Y) = Cov(X,Y) / sqrt[Var(X)Var(Y)].
11. Interpret the correlation (**four-choice question**).

Worked solutions show marginal formulas and support, weighted sums or integrals, exact rational intermediate values, and the final numerical calculations. They distinguish standard deviations from variances in the correlation denominator. The models include strong positive, strong negative, moderate positive, and very weak negative linear association, plus a zero-correlation dependent model and a zero-correlation independent model.

The first continuous problem extends the lecture example f(x,y) = (2/7)(x+2y), 0 < x < 1 and 1 < y < 2. Rectangular support describes the integration bounds; it does not by itself establish statistical independence. Strength descriptions use the lecture’s absolute-correlation rule of thumb, with zero treated separately as no linear association. These categories are descriptive rather than universal cutoffs. Neither covariance nor correlation alone establishes causation.

### Student Instructions and Quiz Behavior

1. Main questions/problems are shuffled without replacement each round. The eleven steps of a calculation problem stay in order.
2. Numerical questions accept **signed decimals, fractions, and scientific notation**. Enter only the requested number, without units or a percent sign. Values may be negative or exceed 1 when appropriate; correlation must lie between -1 and 1.
3. Round final decimal entries to **four decimal places**. Keep exact fractions or full precision between steps, especially for the very small covariance in the lecture example.
4. Derive marginal functions on paper. Their formulas and support appear in the solutions; only the requested numerical moments are automatically graded.
5. Interpretation questions have four options whose display order is randomized. Select exactly one. Each option has explanatory feedback.
6. Each question allows one scored attempt. Blank or invalid input does not use an attempt. **Show Solution** reveals the answer without awarding a point.
7. Correct intermediate answers are shown after checking, so later steps can use them even if an earlier response was wrong. Scores count individual subquestions.
8. Finish the round and restart to reset the score and reshuffle. Reloading the page resets progress; no responses are sent to a server.
