Confidence Intervals for a Population Mean

Choose the method, construct the interval, and interpret it. Four cases, four problems each.

Study guide: choosing an interval

Use n ≥ 30 as the large-sample guideline. All large-sample problems here have n > 30; all small-sample problems have n < 30.

InformationIntervalTI-84 Stats entry
Normal population; known σ, any nx̄ ± z*σ/√n (exact)ZInterval; σ = population SD
Population PMF; n > 30x̄ ± z*σ/√n (CLT approximation)ZInterval; derive population σ from the PMF
Unknown σ; known sample s; n > 30x̄ ± z*s/√n (large-sample approximation)ZInterval; enter s in the σ field
Normal population; unknown σ; n < 30x̄ ± t*s/√n; df = n − 1 (exact)TInterval; Sx = s

The critical value leaves α/2 in each tail, where α = 1 − confidence level. A normal population gives an exact Z interval with known σ even for a small n. Use the standard deviation, not the variance, in the calculator.
